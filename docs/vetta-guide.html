VETTA 01 — WHAT IT DOES
The pipeline

Four steps, in plain order.

Nothing is skipped and nothing is silent: each step writes what it did, and every score names the line of the resume it came from.

01 — DEFINE

Calibrate the role

A short intake turns the job description into a rubric: must-haves, stack, compensation band, work mode, employment type and notice-period reality. That rubric is the only thing the pipeline scores against — no keywords, no pedigree filters.

02 — SOURCE

Fill one desk

Public role pages take applications directly. Every resume — PDF or DOCX — is parsed into a structured profile: current title, employer, years, location, phone, links and the full text. Batches are uploaded, parsed and queued continuously.

03 — SCREEN

Rank, then verify

Each candidate is ranked against the rubric with cited evidence, and their claims are checked by arithmetic and document forensics. A structured written screen follows — asynchronous, no live call — scored with its reasoning attached.

04 — DECIDE

Shortlist, stamp, send

A ranked shortlist with reasoning, client-ready disclosure, approvals and offer support. You approve and stamp; the desk moves the candidate through shortlisted, screening, ready, approved and sent — every transition on the audit trail.

VETTA 03 — HOW IT WORKS UNDER THE HOOD
Method, not magic

Six rules the software is built on.

RULE 01

The rubric decides, not the model's taste

The job description compiles to a rubric — must-haves, stack, band, notice reality. A candidate is scored against that rubric and nothing else, so a school, a former employer or a flattering word cannot move a score on its own.

RULE 02

Every score names its evidence

A number without a citation is an opinion. Each rank, gap and screen score carries the resume or answer line it rests on, so a founder can disagree with the evidence instead of the algorithm.

RULE 03

Verification is arithmetic and bytes

Checks are date subtraction, transaction-chain counting and document forensics — never sentiment. Only a definitive check may contradict a claim; everything else is labelled unverifiable rather than dressed up as doubt.

RULE 04

A human stamps what leaves the building

Vetta drafts. A named person approves. Nothing is sent to a client, and no candidate is marked as decided, without that stamp — and each stamp is written to the audit trail with who and when.

RULE 05

The model layer fails over, never guesses

Inference runs across a ladder of providers with circuit breaking and automatic failover. Each attempt carries a hard timeout and a budget; an arm that is slow or failing is dropped, and its failure never becomes a verdict.

RULE 06

Privacy is a construction, not a setting

Consent is itemized and recorded before processing. Disclosure is filtered when the client reads it, not when it is stored. Erasure deletes the subject's data and recomputes the derived aggregates that had counted them, so nothing quietly survives in a median.

VETTA 04 — HOW LONG IT TAKES
The clock

Machine time is minutes.
Hiring time is people.

Vetta compresses the work that used to be a recruiter's evenings — reading, ranking, drafting, chasing. What it deliberately does not compress is the two places a person has to be: your review, and the candidate's answer.

StepMachine timeWhat it is actually waiting on
Role goes live Immediate on save No model call; the rubric is compiled from the form and the job description.
Resume parsed Seconds per file Native extraction, no inference in the path. Batch uploads queue and drain continuously.
Candidate ranked One model call, hard-capped at ~14s per attempt with failover Queue position in front of it — a 200-resume batch is hours of reading compressed into the time the queue takes to drain.
Screen generated One model call, same cap The click. A draft screen exists before the recruiter finishes reading the verdict.
Candidate answers ~10 minutes per scenario question (bounded 4–25), up to 2 follow-ups each The candidate. Written and asynchronous — no live call to schedule, nothing to attend.
Verdict leaves A few minutes of a person's attention You. Vetta drafts and cites; it will not decide for you.
Apply → shortlist, measured Published weekly, from real timestamps The cohort. Below 5 companies, 25 roles and 50 applications the number is withheld, not guessed.
Why there is no “hiring is 3× faster” number here

Because we have not measured it on enough of your kind of hiring yet. The caps above are engineering bounds — how the system is built to behave — not benchmarks. The one duration Vetta quotes publicly is the median time from applying to being shortlisted, cut weekly from stage timestamps, and it stays off the page until the cohort behind it is large enough to mean something. That rule is the reason a number beside our name is worth reading.
